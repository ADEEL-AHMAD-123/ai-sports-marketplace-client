What EdgeAI does, in one paragraph

For every player prop on the board — like "Aaron Judge over 1.5 total bases" or "LeBron James over 24.5 points" — EdgeAI pulls the last 15+ games of stats, the current sportsbook line, the opposing starter or matchup data, and situational factors like ballpark and platoon splits. It then produces an OVER/UNDER recommendation, a signal-confidence score, and an edge percentage against the sportsbook line. Every pick shows the supporting factors, risk factors, and the AI's plain-language summary so you can decide whether to trust it.

How EdgeAI is different

vs. odds screens (OddsJam, Action Network live odds)

Odds screens tell you the current line at every book, which is useful for line shopping — but they don't tell you whether the line is beatable. EdgeAI adds the "should I take this?" layer with statistical support and confidence scoring.

vs. tipster services

Most tipster services give you the pick without showing why. EdgeAI shows the full reasoning: the stats, the matchup, the edge math, and the risk factors. You're never asked to trust a black box.

vs. spreadsheet grinders

You could compile all of this yourself in a spreadsheet — pulling stat splits, matchup context, and ballpark data for every player prop. EdgeAI just makes it 30 seconds instead of an hour, and does it consistently across five sports.

EdgeAI is a decision-support tool, not a lock service. No analysis tool can guarantee outcomes in sports — variance is real. What EdgeAI can do is show you the data-driven case for each pick so you're not betting on gut feel.

How it makes money

EdgeAI uses prepaid credits. You buy a pack (starting at $2.99 for three insights) and spend one credit each time you unlock a scouting report. There's no monthly subscription, no minimums, and credits never expire. Bigger packs scale cheaper per credit — see the pricing page for the full breakdown.

Data sources

EdgeAI pulls from official league APIs — MLB Stats API, NHL Stats API, NBA public data, and API-Sports for soccer and NFL — for game logs and player stats. Sportsbook lines come from The Odds API, which aggregates prices from major regulated books.
